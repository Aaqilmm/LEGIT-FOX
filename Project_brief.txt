============================================================ 1. PROJECT
PURPOSE AND SIH PROBLEM
============================================================

The project addresses SIH problem statement SIH26264: an integrated
dashboard for monitoring and tracking institution-related complaint
cases across AICTE Bureaus/Cells.

AICTE may receive complaints concerning the same institution through
different Bureaus/Cells, including Regulation Bureau (RB), Public
Grievance Redressal Cell (PGRC), and other relevant units. When cases
are handled independently, officials may have limited visibility into
related cases, overlapping actions, repeated document requests, delays,
case ownership, and final outcomes.

LEGIT is a coordinated grievance-intelligence platform with two
connected user experiences:

A. STUDENT EXPERIENCE — “Legit Fox” A friendly portal where students
can: - Explain an issue in natural language. - Understand what
information is needed to submit a useful complaint. - Check whether
essential details or evidence are missing. - Receive simple, reasoned
suggestions for improving a complaint. - Understand possible grievance
categories and likely official routes. - Ask questions through an AI
assistant that retrieves answers from verified regulatory/procedural
documents when those documents are configured. - Prepare a factual
complaint draft without inventing allegations. - Discover anonymized,
relevant historical case summaries and recorded outcomes, where
available. - Track a complaint only when an actual authorized
submission/integration exists. In this prototype, clearly label any
submission/tracking process as simulated.

B. OFFICIAL EXPERIENCE — “AICTE Legit” An authorized-official dashboard
where users can: - See consolidated complaint activity by institution. -
Search institution profiles and complaint records. - View the
responsible Bureau/Cell, owner, status, timeline, next action, due date,
and recorded outcome. - Detect potential cross-Bureau overlaps and
related complaints. - Review, confirm, or dismiss suggested
relationships with a reason. - Coordinate across Bureaus using requests,
responses, ownership, and action history. - Monitor overdue cases,
inactivity, deadlines, escalations, workload, and disposal trends. -
Search and filter by institution, AICTE Permanent ID, case ID,
Bureau/Cell, region, category, status, and dates. - Retrieve similar
historical cases and verified recorded actions/outcomes to support, not
replace, official judgement. - Generate reports and inspect audit
history.

The core value proposition: “LEGIT understands complaints expressed in
different words, identifies potential cross-Bureau overlaps, checks
complaint completeness before submission, and retrieves verified
historical actions and outcomes to help students and authorized
officials handle grievances more efficiently.”

The official dashboard and inter-Bureau coordination are the core SIH
solution. The student chatbot and quality checker are supporting
innovations, not a replacement for official grievance processes.

============================================================ 2.
CONFIRMED PRODUCT IDEAS — MUST BE IMPLEMENTED
============================================================

FEATURE 1 — SEMANTIC GRIEVANCE MATCHING Complaints can describe the same
underlying issue using different wording. Example synthetic examples: -
“My college is keeping my original certificates.” - “The institution
refuses to return my academic documents.” - “I graduated but still
cannot collect my original certificates.”

The system should be designed to compare meanings, not only exact
keywords. In the current UI/demo phase: - Create a Similar Cases panel
showing ranked candidates, similarity score/label, matching reasons,
institution, Bureau, status, and case ID. - Explain why a case was
suggested, such as matching issue type, institution, key facts, or
semantic similarity. - Separate exact institution matching from semantic
issue similarity. - Treat similarity as a signal, never proof that two
complaints are duplicates. - Never automatically merge, close, reject,
or cancel complaints. - Let authorized officials confirm, mark as
related, mark as duplicate, or dismiss a suggested match with a
reason. - Keep unrelated complaints for the same institution separate. -
Related complaints from different institutions may be useful as
historical examples but must not be falsely linked as the same case. -
Use synthetic examples until a real database and embedding service are
configured.

FEATURE 2 — HISTORICAL RESOLUTION INTELLIGENCE When a case is closed,
the authorized officer should record structured information about the
outcome and actions taken. A future similar case can retrieve those
records as potentially useful examples.

Provide a structured case-closure form with: - Outcome category. -
Summary of the final decision/action. - Actions taken. - Whether the
issue was fully resolved, partially resolved, not substantiated,
referred elsewhere, withdrawn, procedurally closed, unresolved at
closure, or another configured category. - Evidence/reference notes. -
Closure date. - Reviewing/closing official. - Optional follow-up or
compliance check. - Verification state and verifier.

Rules: - A closed case is not automatically a successful resolution. - A
historical action is not a legal precedent and is not guaranteed to
apply to a new case. - Only verified outcomes should be treated as
trusted historical guidance. - Do not automatically retrain a model or
create new legal rules from every closure. In the future backend, index
verified outcomes for retrieval. - Show the user why a historical case
may be relevant and include a caveat that applicability depends on facts
and current rules. - In demo mode, make this workflow functional with
local mock state and clearly label data as synthetic.

FEATURE 3 — SMART EVIDENCE & COMPLAINT QUALITY CHECKER This is a
supportive completeness checker, not an automatic rejection engine.

The checker should: - Ask simple, relevant follow-up questions based on
the complaint category and details supplied. - Identify missing
information, unclear dates, unclear institution identity, missing event
chronology, or evidence/document references that may help an official
assess the issue. - Explain in plain language why each requested detail
is useful. - Separate required fields from optional supporting
details. - Provide a short checklist with states such as Complete,
Needed, and Optional. - Offer a simple “Improve my complaint” flow that
helps the student add missing details. - Re-check the complaint after
edits and show progress. - Explain that a missing attachment does not
always mean a complaint is invalid; the official rules/process determine
actual requirements. - Never automatically discard or reject a complaint
because the checker reports missing information. - Never encourage a
student to fabricate evidence or add unsupported allegations. - Allow “I
don’t have this information” or “Not applicable” where appropriate. -
Keep the process low-effort, accessible, and respectful. - The official
decision on acceptance/validity belongs to the authorized process, not
the AI checker.

FEATURE 4 — CROSS-BUREAU COORDINATION INTELLIGENCE - Display potential
overlaps where an institution has multiple active cases across
Bureaus/Cells. - Show the reason for each overlap suggestion and
distinguish same-institution matching from issue similarity. - Show
current owner, Bureau, status, last action, next action, and expected
disposal date. - Support a coordination request, responses, assigned
responsibilities, due dates, and an action log. - Let authorized users
accept, reject, or defer a suggested relationship, recording who made
the decision and why. - Detect possible conflicting or repetitive
actions only as suggestions; do not assert conflict without review. -
Never automatically reassign or merge cases based solely on AI output.

FEATURE 5 — VERIFIED REGULATORY ASSISTANT / RAG FOUNDATION The student
assistant should be prepared for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG): -
Retrieve relevant passages from a configured collection of verified,
current official regulations, procedures, circulars, and guidance. -
Display document title and section/provision/page or other available
source metadata beside answers. - Clearly distinguish regulatory source
material from anonymized historical case examples. - If no relevant
source is available, say so rather than inventing a legal answer. - Do
not fabricate laws, sections, deadlines, rights, or official
processes. - Avoid presenting output as binding legal advice or a final
official decision. - Allow a future Gemini API integration through the
server only. Never expose the Gemini API key in frontend code. - For
now, implement a typed assistant service interface and a demo response
mode. Do not pretend that RAG is live if the source corpus or API is not
configured. - The assistant can help categorize a complaint and explain
possible routes, but it must not route every issue to PGRC
automatically. Routing rules must be configurable and verified against
official guidance.
